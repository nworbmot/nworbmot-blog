#+TITLE: Is AI coming for theoretical physics?
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil

First Posted: 2026.11.22, [[https://github.com/nworbmot/nworbmot-blog][Revision History]], Author: [[https://www.nworbmot.org/][Tom Brown]]

[[https://lnkd.in/p/dCmEz27S][Parallel LinkedIn post]]

As the [[https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/][Riemann hypothesis falls]], will theoretical physics be the next
field to experience AI disruption?  Like mathematics, theoretical
physics relies more on internal consistency than verification from the
real world, and has gone to places inaccessible to experiment in its
quest to unify general relativity with quantum theory.

The situation in theoretical physics is summed up very nicely in this
article by Dan Kagan-Kans, supported by multiple interviews with the
big beasts of the field:

[[https://www.understandingai.org/p/i-spoke-to-seven-physics-experts][I spoke to seven physics experts grappling with the AI revolution]]

In 2026 the field has seen a 30% uptick in papers submitted to
the preprint server arXiv's theoretical physics category hep-th.

As a recovering theoretical physicist, I'm mostly excited to see what
this new tool of discovery brings. I'd be thrilled to read a
microscopic proof of the [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AdS/CFT_correspondence][AdS/CFT correspondence]] along the lines
explored by [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13397][Gopakumar and colleagues]] or [[https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0703282][Berkovits]]. There are several
[[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole_information_paradox][black hole information paradoxes]] to resolve.

The field was already flooded by papers reporting results of the
esoteric micro-increment variety, to the extent that back in 2010
[[https://davidsd.org/][David Simmons-Duffin]] built a snarXiv of automatically generated paper
titles. The game was to guess which titles were real versus invented,
and most of us scored poorly:

[[https://snarxiv.org/vs-arxiv/][arXiv or snarXiv?]]

So filtering by personal reputation has always been a necessity.

For my current field of energy research there are [[https://nworbmot.org/blog/biomass-efficiently.html#changes][very concrete
problems]] that AI could help solve.

AI material discovery could bring batteries with a cell density above
800 Wh/kg made for under 300 EUR/kWh with abundant materials, which
would give us regional electric aircraft. Stable manufacturing
processes for solid oxide fuel cells that bring the cost under 1500
EUR/kW_el would relieve the gas turbine crunch and revolutionise
long-duration storage.

If we [[https://tupress.temple.edu/books/frontiers-of-illusion][keep focused on what allows us to live better]] and harness the
technology to that end, then the future is bright.
