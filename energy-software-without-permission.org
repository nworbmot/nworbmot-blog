#+TITLE: Energy and Software Without Permission
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil

First Posted: 2026.11.02, [[https://github.com/nworbmot/nworbmot-blog][Revision History]], Author: [[https://www.nworbmot.org/][Tom Brown]]

[[https://lnkd.in/p/dcdiGw5Q][Parallel LinkedIn post]]

Europe's dependence on proprietary software resembles its dependence
on fossil fuels: it spends hundreds of billions of euros a year on
foreign suppliers, and disruption threatens daily life. But for both
there are now great alternatives: open source software on one side,
renewables and electrification on the other.

A [[https://www.cigref.fr/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/TECHNOLOGICAL-DEPENDENCE-ON-AMERICAN-SOFTWARE-AND-CLOUD-SERVICES-AN-ASSESSMENT-OF-THE-ECONOMIC-CONSEQUENCES.pdf][2025 study by Asterès]] put the annual spending on proprietary IT
services at around 264 billion EUR from the US alone. After US
President Donald Trump sanctioned the International Criminal Court in
February 2025, its chief prosecutor [[https://www.heise.de/en/news/Criminal-Court-Microsoft-s-email-block-a-wake-up-call-for-digital-sovereignty-10387383.html][reportedly lost access to his
Microsoft email]]. In June 2026, foreign nationals were [[https://www.politico.com/news/2026/06/15/trumps-newest-fight-with-anthropic-00962858][barred from
using Anthropic's latest AI models]]. Do other countries want this
kind of dependency when we might have serious foreign policy
disagreements with the White House?

In 2025 the EU [[https://energy.ec.europa.eu/strategy/repowereu-phase-out-russian-energy-imports/repowereu-4-years_en][spent 335 billion EUR]] on fossil fuels, mostly
imported. Besides the enormous climate and health damage they
cause, we are extremely vulnerable to supply interruptions. The gas
crisis after the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and
the aftermath of the US-Israeli attack on Iran showed this.

Fortunately there are solutions at hand for both problems.

There are now universal technologies for cheap electricity that every
country can deploy: [[https://nworbmot.org/blog/solar-battery-world.html][solar, wind and batteries]]. With [[https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/the-electrotech-revolution/][electrification
technologies]] like low-cost electric vehicles and heat pumps, oil and
gas imports can be rapidly reduced. Some of these technologies may be
imported, but they can in principle be produced anywhere, and they
continue working even if there's a supply disruption.

For software, open source packages like [[https://www.libreoffice.org/][LibreOffice]], [[https://nextcloud.com/][Nextcloud]] and
[[https://ubuntu.com/][Ubuntu]] have matured to the point where they can be widely deployed to
replace proprietary alternatives. Mistral offers [[https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4-oss/][open-weight AI
models]]. The EU's [[https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/communication-european-tech-sovereignty-accompanied-eu-open-source-strategy][Tech Sovereignty Package]] has put [[https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/open-source-strategy][Open Source]] at its
centre, next to chip production and accelerating the building of
domestic data centres. Cloud software can be hosted by independent
providers and you can choose your own support. If the original
supplier withdraws, the software can still be run and maintained by
someone else. Open formats and interfaces make it easier to migrate to
another tool if you don't like the current one. In 2022 Europe had [[https://www.linuxfoundation.org/blog/whats-the-state-of-open-source-in-europe-and-why-does-it-matter-now][3
million open source contributors]].

Finally, the benefits of Europe switching to open source have huge
positive spillovers for the whole world. Just like solar deployment in
Europe [[https://www.routledge.com/How-Solar-Energy-Became-Cheap-Pathway-to-a-Solar-Centric-Economy/Nemet/p/book/9781032597492][drove down the costs of solar for everyone]] and pioneer
countries like Denmark [[https://stateofgreen.com/en/][found innovative solutions for renewable
integration]], wider adoption of open source can bring more funding
and contributions to improve the code. Europe can use its purchasing
power to fund improvements that flow back into a shared code base.
