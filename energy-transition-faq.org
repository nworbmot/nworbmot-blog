* Materials for the energy transition

** Doesn't the energy transition just replace fossil fuel consumption with mineral and metal consumption?
  :PROPERTIES:
  :CUSTOM_ID: minerals
  :END:

Today billions of tons of coal, gas and oil are consumed. Energy
transition technologies do not consume fuels, but need metals and
minerals such as silicon for solar panels or lithium for electric
vehicle and stationary batteries.

** Doesn't the energy transition just replace a dependence on fossil fuels exporters with one on green technology exporters like China?
  :PROPERTIES:
  :CUSTOM_ID: import-dependence
  :END:

There are several points to make here:

- The dependency on green technology is much less immediate because solar panels and batteries are one-time purchases that last for decades. If there is a trade blockade, they will continue to generate electricity, whereas a fuel blockade will create an imminent emergency.
- The manufacturing capacity can be built anywhere. Much of the capacity has been built in China because China can produce more cheaply, but other countries could built up their own capacity if they want to maintain resilience.
- Green tech prices show much lower volatility than fossil fuel prices.
- More than half of the value (installation, permitting, grid connection) stays local.

** Does the energy transition really improve the trade balance if we just substitute importing fossil fuels for importing green technology?
  :PROPERTIES:
  :CUSTOM_ID: trade-balance
  :END:

- For PV the modules themselves are ~20-40% of the total installed cost. Mounting, labor, development, permitting, grid connection all stay domestic. SOmetimes the inverter can also be produced locally (e.g. in Europe by SMA, Fronius). For rooftop the local value is even higher and the module fraction is lower.

** Aren't the critical mineral resources concentrated in particular countries?
  :PROPERTIES:
  :CUSTOM_ID: resource-concentration
  :END:

Some critical minerals are concentrated in particular countries, like cobalt in the Democratic Republic of Congo, but very few are essential for the energy transition because their use can be reduced over time or substituted altogether.

Lithium-ion batteries with cobalt and manganese (LMC) can be substituted with Lithium-iron phosphate (LFP).

Lithium efficiency increase all the time.

The lithium in lithium-ion batteries itself can be substituted with sodium, which are slightly less dense in volume and mass. Already cars and stationary storage use sodium.
